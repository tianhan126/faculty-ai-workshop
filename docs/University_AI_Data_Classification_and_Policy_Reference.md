# University AI Data Classification & Policy Reference

*Curated public reference for the DKU Faculty Workshop: Agentic Workflow with Codex (compiled November 2026).*  
*Disclaimer: This document summarizes publicly available university policies and guidelines for faculty awareness and cross-institutional comparison. It is **not** formal compliance guidance or institutional authorization. For specific DKU tool permissions, data handling rules, or system integrations, please consult DKU CTL, IT, and the Office of the Registrar.*

---

## 1. Practical Principles Used in This Workshop

Across universities, AI governance is rarely a blanket "all allowed" or "all banned" rule. Instead, institutions evaluate AI use across four dimensions: **data sensitivity**, **intended purpose**, **institutional agreement / account environment**, and **system integration permissions**.

To keep hands-on experimentation safe and clear for all disciplines, this workshop follows three baseline principles:

1. **Start with public, mock, or non-sensitive materials:** Practice workflows using public documents, synthetic files, or your own non-sensitive drafts before considering real course or research records.
2. **Separate model access from system authorization:** Using a university-supported API or institutional account provides an approved model pathway, but it does not automatically authorize connecting external plugins or live campus systems (such as Canvas or university email).
3. **Verify classification before sharing records:** Materials that identify individual students, research participants, or confidential collaborators are governed by institutional privacy, ethics (IRB), and copyright rules regardless of file format (PDF, Word, CSV, or email).

## 3. Key Concepts Frequently Raised by Faculty

* **Education Records (FERPA) vs. Blank Assignments:** Under U.S. FERPA standards, an *education record* is defined by who it directly relates to and who maintains it—not whether the topic feels personally private. Blank assignment prompts or synthetic examples are not student education records; identifiable student submissions, grades, and comments maintained by faculty or an LMS generally are. Simply deleting a student's name may not fully de-identify a file if metadata, filenames, or text details remain identifiable. ([U.S. Dept. of Education FAQ](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/what-education-record))
* **PHI (Protected Health Information):** PHI refers specifically to identifiable health, medical, and healthcare payment information regulated under HIPAA (e.g., clinical records). Non-PHI does not mean unrestricted—education records and confidential research data carry separate protections. ([HHS HIPAA Overview](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/laws-regulations/index.html))
* **Model Training vs. Context & Memory:** Institutional AI workspaces and API gateways generally disable model training on institutional inputs by default. Disabling training does **not** remove the agent's ability to read your project folder, follow instructions, or hold context within a working session. However, switching between a personal ChatGPT login and an institutional API key does not automatically transfer personal chat history or account-level memory. ([OpenAI API Data Controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data))
* **Research Materials & Authorship:** "Personal data" in research data policies refers to personally identifiable information about people (such as interview participants or students), not simply "my own research drafts." At the same time, sharing co-authored unpublished manuscripts, licensed publisher PDFs, or peer-review manuscripts requires respecting co-authors, database licenses, IRB protocols, and confidentiality agreements. ([UChicago Library Appropriate Use Guide](https://guides.lib.uchicago.edu/GenAI/appropriate-use))
